The challenge

The business was locked into a site it didn't control

What I inherited

  • A five-page Duda template bundled with Housecall Pro, with booking, chat and reviews all running as third-party widgets.
  • The domain sat under the old provider's control, with nameservers pointed at an AWS Route 53 zone nobody at Delta could log into.
  • A headline ("Delivering Excellence, One Service at a Time") with no service or town in it, and no structured data at all.
  • Template leftovers: four Tennessee towns in the service-area list, a "We delivers" typo, weekend hours listed two different ways, and social icons linked to generic home pages.

What the owner needed

  • To show up when someone in Red River, Angel Fire or Eagle Nest searches for an electrician or a standby generator.
  • To own his domain and site independently of any one software vendor.
  • Leads routed into Buildertrend, the project software he already runs the business on.
  • A fast, simple page that works on a phone with one bar of signal, with calling one tap away.
  • A budget-conscious build: packages ran $850–$2,500, with Webflow hosting costs shown openly.

Process

Two weeks, discovery call to DNS cutover

I ran it as a small phased project: scope and price it, audit, design, build, migrate, tune, hand off.

Deliverables handed to the client

  • Build checklist and phased plan from the discovery call
  • Two pricing sheets (PDF) with a transparent Webflow cost breakdown
  • 12-page brand & content reference extracted from the old site
  • Launched Webflow site with SEO settings and schema
  • Domain & Account Summary (PDF): registrar login, 2FA backup codes, DNS records and authenticator setup
  • 301 redirect map and DNS cutover runbook

  1. Discovery

    Scoping & pricing

    Turned a recorded call with the owner into a build checklist, three phases (domain recovery, content, build/launch) and pricing tiers.

  2. Audit

    Content & brand extraction

    Pulled copy, colors (navy #0A2545, gold #F5B502, light #EFF6F9) and Open Sans type specs from all five pages into a reference PDF, and logged every defect to fix.

  3. Platform

    Wix tried, Webflow chosen

    A Wix import came back as an editorless headless site. I compared Webflow, Framer, WordPress.com and Sanity on security certifications and editing, and picked Webflow.

  4. UX / UI

    One mobile-first landing page

    Sticky header with click-to-call, hero, three service cards, Why Us, service areas, FAQ accordion, contact and footer. Placed the client's navy and white logos by context.

  5. SEO

    Copy rewritten around local search

    New H1, keyword-led service headings, town names in body copy, seven local FAQs. Removed an unconfirmed "free estimate" claim before it could go live.

  6. Build

    Native Webflow elements

    Built section by section as editable Webflow elements with scoped de- classes, then added meta, Open Graph and JSON-LD.

  7. Domain

    Recovery & transfer

    Moved the domain from the old provider to Namecheap, with 2FA, domain lock and WHOIS privacy, and the owner set as the domain contact.

  8. Network

    DNS cutover

    Checked MX records first, switched nameservers off Route 53, added the Webflow records, verified both hostnames and set www as primary.

  9. QA

    Tune, test, launch

    Embedded the Buildertrend form, fixed layout shift, verified every legacy URL redirects, and ran Lighthouse until mobile hit 98.

Information architecture

Five thin pages became one long page with anchor navigation (#services, #why, #areas, #faq, #contact). Visitors scroll on a phone instead of waiting for new pages to load on a weak signal.

Visual system

I kept the existing brand so returning customers recognize it: navy and gold, Open Sans 400–800, gold-topped service cards, and hover states on every button and nav link.

Accessible by default

Domain & network engineering

Getting the domain back, then a clean DNS cutover

The old provider had taken ownership of the domain, and its DNS lived in an AWS Route 53 zone under their account. Anything added at the registrar would have been ignored. I moved the domain to Namecheap, planned the cutover so email couldn't break, and switched with the new site already waiting.

Cutover runbook

  1. Registrar choice

    Compared Namecheap, Porkbun and Cloudflare. Cloudflare needs its own nameservers before it accepts a transfer, which conflicted with the old provider's process, so I chose Namecheap.

  2. Transfer & harden

    Transferred the domain, then turned on registrar 2FA, domain lock, auto-renew and WHOIS privacy. Checked status through WHOIS/RDAP.

  3. Protect email first

    Ran MX and TXT lookups before touching nameservers. The business email is a standalone Gmail account, so no mail records had to be carried over.

  4. Switch nameservers

    Moved from the four awsdns servers to Namecheap BasicDNS and deleted the parking records Namecheap adds by default.

  5. Verify & publish

    Watched propagation on DNSChecker, verified both hostnames in Webflow, set www as primary, published, and let Webflow issue SSL.

Performance & QA

Chasing layout shift down to a 98

The first post-launch Lighthouse run flagged a high Cumulative Layout Shift. I traced it to two causes and fixed both at the source rather than hiding them.

Reserved logo space

The header logo had no fixed dimensions, so everything below it moved about 33px when it loaded. I gave it an explicit 88 × 77 box set to Contain, matching the triangle mark's shape, at every breakpoint.

Font swap removed

I replaced Webflow's Google Fonts loader with preconnects and a single Open Sans request using display=optional, and dropped an unused weight. Text no longer re-wraps once the web font arrives.

Lighter form embed

I rewrote Buildertrend's snippet: the script is deferred and placed after the iframe, the iframe lazy-loads and starts with a reserved height, and its width is capped at 760px. Then I sent a real test submission through to Buildertrend.

QA checklist run before handoff

  • Lighthouse mobile run three times after publishing
  • Desktop, tablet, mobile landscape and portrait breakpoints
  • One H1, ordered H2/H3 outline, crawlable FAQ
  • tel: and mailto: links on every contact point
  • Every legacy URL redirects; www is canonical over HTTPS
  • Template leftovers, typos and conflicting hours fixed
  • Buildertrend form test lead received
  • Structured data checked against the visible copy

Next round: add a <main> landmark (the one open Accessibility item), set explicit sizes on the remaining logo images, and trim preconnects to the four origins that matter.
